Computing's Impact on Healthcare

Computers have changed all aspects of healthcare, from spreadsheeting to early diagnoses.

Data storage

Electronic health records

Electronic health records (EHRs) store a patient's medical history, medication, allergies, test results and previous treatments digitally. Authorised healthcare professionals can access this information without relying on paper records.

This reduces paperwork, prevents information from being lost and helps doctors make more informed decisions.

Example

A patient visits a hospital they have not used before. Staff may be able to access their records electronically instead of obtaining physical documents from another healthcare provider.
Scanning

Medical imaging

Computers are essential for producing and analysing medical images from X-rays, CT scans and MRI scans. Computer systems convert measurements collected by the scanning equipment into detailed images that doctors can examine.

Advanced computing can also help identify abnormalities in these images. AI systems can be trained to recognise patterns linked to conditions such as cancer, potentially helping clinicians detect diseases earlier.

Support, not replacement

This technology is intended to support healthcare professionals rather than replace their judgement.
Machine learning

Artificial intelligence and diagnosis

AI can process very large amounts of medical information and spot patterns that may be difficult to detect manually. Machine-learning systems can assist with analysing scans, predicting risks and identifying patients who may need further investigation.

This can contribute to earlier diagnosis and more personalised treatment.

The risk

AI systems need to be carefully tested, because inaccurate or biased results could negatively affect patients.
Remote care

Telemedicine

Computing and the internet let patients communicate with healthcare professionals remotely through video consultations, messaging and online appointment systems.

This is particularly useful for people who live far from healthcare facilities, have mobility difficulties or need routine follow-up appointments.

The benefit

Telemedicine makes healthcare more accessible while reducing the need for some patients to travel.
Sensors

Wearable technology

Smartwatches and other wearable devices use sensors to collect information such as heart rate, activity levels and other health-related measurements. This helps individuals monitor their health and, in some circumstances, gives useful information to healthcare professionals.

Example

Continuous monitoring can help identify changes that might need further medical attention.

This shows how hardware, software, data analysis and communication networks work together in modern healthcare.
