Computing's Impact on Healthcare

Electronic health records

Electronic health records (EHRs) allow information such as a patient's medical history, medication, allergies, test results and previous treatments to be stored digitally. This means authorised healthcare professionals can access relevant information without relying on paper records. It can reduce paperwork, prevent information from being lost and help doctors make more informed decisions. For example, if a patient visits a hospital that they have not used before, staff may be able to access relevant records electronically rather than having to obtain physical documents from another healthcare provider.

Medical imaging

Computers are essential for producing and analysing medical images from technologies such as X-rays, CT scans and MRI scans. Computer systems convert measurements collected by scanning equipment into detailed images that doctors can examine. Advanced computing can also help identify abnormalities in these images. AI systems can be trained to recognise patterns associated with conditions such as cancer, potentially helping clinicians detect diseases earlier. The technology is intended to support healthcare professionals rather than replace their judgement.

Artificial intelligence and diagnosis

Artificial intelligence can process very large amounts of medical information and identify patterns that may be difficult to detect manually. Machine-learning systems can assist with tasks such as analysing scans, predicting risks and identifying patients who may require further investigation. This can contribute to earlier diagnosis and more personalised treatment. However, AI systems need to be carefully tested because inaccurate or biased results could negatively affect patients.

Telemedicine

Computing and the internet allow patients to communicate with healthcare professionals remotely through video consultations, messaging and online appointment systems. This is particularly useful for people who live far from healthcare facilities, have mobility difficulties or need routine follow-up appointments. Telemedicine can therefore make healthcare more accessible while reducing the need for some patients to travel.

Wearable technology

Smartwatches and other wearable devices can use sensors to collect information such as heart rate, activity levels and other health-related measurements. This information can help individuals monitor aspects of their health and, in some circumstances, provide useful information to healthcare professionals. For example, continuous monitoring can help identify changes that might require further medical attention. This demonstrates how hardware, software, data analysis and communication networks work together in modern healthcare.
